# Broadcast acceptance plan

These numerical limits are proposed pass/fail thresholds, not measurements. The owner and providers must approve or revise them before the test begins. Record immutable release and configuration fingerprints, input frame rate, approved assets, clock accuracy, receiver implementation and sampling intervals. Use independent receivers for each declared output, plus transport timestamps, reference audio markers and synchronized logs.

| Test | Proposed acceptance threshold and evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Endurance and quality | Both channels deliver 1920 × 1080 H.264/AAC concurrently for 72 hours. Validate every rolling decoding interval and full stream timestamps. Each encoder meets the configured frame rate and 4–6 Mbps target within 15% over five-minute windows unless a different provider profile is approved. Zero unplanned service restarts or unrecovered output failures. |
| Camera rotation and sound | Cover every configured camera/dwell interval and manual switch. Start the next healthy camera within one second of its target after preroll. Zero encoder restarts or decoded independent-audio gaps over 100 ms during routine switches. Fail one source, skip to the next healthy source within five seconds, use fallback within five seconds if all fail and rejoin an eligible recovered source within 15 seconds of sustained health. |
| Movies, schedules and ads | Play at least one representative full-length movie end to end and a 72-hour published timeline. Check automatic next item, recurrence, priority, fallback and each scheduled ad against as-run and decoder timestamps within one second. Reject deletion of every active or scheduled test asset. |
| Continuity and sync | Keep the same logical provider/channel endpoint through camera, audio, movie, ad and schedule changes and recoverable input loss. No receiver rebuffer over one second in routine transitions on the agreed reference receiver. Absolute A/V offset at most 80 ms at p95 and 150 ms maximum on synchronized fixture content. Record gap and freeze events separately for each output. |
| Recovery and updates | Fenced takeover after a killed worker within 15 seconds, rejoin both channels within 120 seconds after a full-host restart, and restore data plus referenced media to an isolated host with proposed RPO 15 minutes and RTO 60 minutes. API-only update and rollback within the one-second routine receiver budget. Worker updates require the approved handoff path and their own gap measurement. Verify identities and schedules after each action. |
| Provider access | Verify valid HLS manifests and every segment type plus approved MPEG-TS transport. Zero successful unauthorized, wrong-channel, revoked or disallowed-IP test requests, including direct paths. Revocation takes effect on new requests within five seconds and active connection behavior follows the approved provider contract. |
| Upload under load | Concurrently resume two uploads of at least 10 GB each after interruption, verify final digest, codec and duration, and stay inside the same receiver budgets. Enforce quotas and document completion throughput. |
| Resource headroom | On approved deployment hardware, p95 CPU and GPU utilization each at most 75% of allocated capacity, RAM usage at most 80%, media+scratch usage below 80%, p95 network utilization at most 75% and no sustained growth or OOM. Record CPU, RAM, GPU/VRAM, storage throughput/latency, network drops and channel/provider bandwidth. Record hardware fallback evidence separately. |

Use controlled fault injection and keep recovery windows distinct from routine transitions and endurance. Close release only when security, recovery and playback evidence are accepted. See [update and rollback](UPDATE_AND_ROLLBACK_PLAN.md).
